Everyone thinks

Some of the smartest people in your city will never be called intellectuals. Here’s why that matters.

In the 1930s, writing from a prison cell, Antonio Gramsci argued that everyone thinks, but only some people are given the title of thinker.

Patricia Hill Collins, a Black feminist scholar, took it further. She showed that Black women have long built knowledge in families, churches, and community work, even when universities didn’t call it theory.

Thinkers in your city

Today that looks like the tenant organizer who knows the housing code by heart, or the mother who has read every school board policy. Their work is often unpaid and uncredited.

Crediting the people who do the work

Here are three steps I’m trying to take.

One. Pick one issue where you live.

Two. Find who’s already organizing around it, and notice whose work goes unnamed.

Three. Ask what they need, take on one task, and credit the people who taught you.

I’m a grad student, still learning to listen first. Tell me whose work in your community deserves more credit.
