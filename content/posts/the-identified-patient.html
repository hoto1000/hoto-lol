What I learned

Something I learned in therapy school that changed how I see the world. Part one.

In family therapy, there’s a term called the identified patient. It’s the one person a family brings in as the problem. A lot of the time it’s a kid. The kid who yells, skips class, or won’t listen.

Family therapists are trained to ask a different question. What is this kid carrying for everyone else? Sometimes the acting out is the loudest sign that the whole house is under stress. Money, a parent working nights, a loss nobody talks about.

Once I learned this, I started seeing it outside the therapy room. Schools do it. News stories do it. We point at one kid, one family, or one group and call them the problem, and we skip asking what they’re carrying.

Three things I’m trying

I’m still working on this myself. Here are three things I’m trying.

One. When someone gets called the problem, I ask what’s going on around them before I judge.

Two. I look at the pressure first, like rent, work hours, and school funding.

Three. When I talk about a group in the news, I try to name the system around them too.

  • The “identified patient” idea comes from family systems therapy and is associated with Don D. Jackson and Virginia Satir (for example, Satir’s Conjoint Family Therapy, 1964).
