What I learned

About half of American adults say they’ve felt lonely. In therapy school, I learned that says a lot about how we live, and less about who we are.

I’m a therapy grad student, and I’m still learning. So take this as me thinking out loud.

In 2023, the U.S. Surgeon General, Dr. Vivek Murthy, put out a report called Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation. It said being disconnected can be as hard on your health as smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day.

It also found that in 2020, Americans spent about 20 minutes a day in person with friends. Nearly twenty years earlier, it was about an hour. For people 15 to 24, time with friends dropped about 70 percent. 2020 was a pandemic year, so keep that in mind. It’s still a big drop.

One idea from couples therapy I keep thinking about comes from Dr. Sue Johnson. With Les Greenberg, she developed Emotionally Focused Therapy. It’s built on attachment theory from the psychiatrist John Bowlby. The idea is that needing other people is how humans are built. Leaning on someone is healthy.

One study shows it. In 2006, James Coan and his team put 16 married women in a brain scanner and told them they might get an electric shock. Holding their husband’s hand calmed the threat response in their brains. A stranger’s hand helped too, just less. And the happier the marriage, the calmer some of those areas got.

So when we tell people to handle everything alone, we’re asking them to go against how they’re wired. A lot of us grew up hearing “don’t be a burden.” We move away for school or work. We say “I’m fine” when we’re not.

It frustrates me that we treat loneliness like a personal flaw. But I have hope, because connection is something we can practice. I see people do it every day, like the neighbor who waves, or the friend who texts “thinking of you.”

Three things I’m trying

Here are three things I’m trying.

One. Text one person just to say I’m thinking of them.

Two. When I’m struggling, tell one person the truth before I say “I’m fine.”

Three. Say yes to one small in-person thing this week, even when staying home is easier.

Who’s someone you could reach out to today?
