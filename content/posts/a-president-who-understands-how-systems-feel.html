Welcome to Project Hoto. This is my personal, unofficial commentary, not an AOC campaign message.

If Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez chooses to run for president in 2028, what should voters look for in deciding whether she understands how systems affect people's lives? I would begin with social location. It describes how a person’s place in society, shaped by family, class, neighborhood, education, work, and identity, can influence what they notice about the systems around them.

Her official biography describes her Bronx roots, her family’s move in search of stronger public schools, her student loans, and her work as a waitress and bartender. It says that after her father’s death from cancer, medical bills and other growing expenses put their home at risk of foreclosure. Her House biography gives me a starting point. It cannot qualify her for office on its own. Those details alone cannot establish whether someone would be a good president or make one person representative of everyone who shares parts of that background. They can shape the questions a leader brings to power: Who can afford a home? Who gets heard? How can a school’s resources depend so much on a ZIP code?

Her record gives me something beyond biography to consider. She has advocated for public and affordable housing. Her House office’s housing page lists some of those proposals. The test is whether proposals are workable, who benefits, who pays, and whether people affected have a voice. Her official House page says she joined the Energy & Commerce Committee in January 2025 and was appointed to three subcommittees: Energy, Health, and Oversight & Investigations. The committee page lists those assignments.

A fair counterargument is that a candidate’s background cannot stand in for governing skill, and it can lead people to assume she speaks for everyone with similar experiences. A president also works through institutions and coalitions, so biography or empathy alone cannot show what policy choices will do.

I don’t think there is one set of “today’s American values.” We’re a diverse country, and people disagree. I believe many of us want leaders who listen, explain their choices, and make institutions answerable to ordinary people. Some voters may see her public voice and policy priorities as relevant evidence; others may weigh different priorities. Voters should still test her plans, judgment, coalition-building, and ability to govern.

What I still don't know

How would her proposals work in practice, and who would pay for them? What evidence would show that a policy reaches the people it is meant to serve? How would she build a coalition when voters and lawmakers disagree about priorities?
