Welcome to Project Hoto. This is my personal, unofficial commentary, not an AOC campaign message.

If Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez chooses to run for president in 2028, I’d begin by thinking about social location. It describes how a person’s place in society, shaped by family, class, neighborhood, education, work, and identity, can influence what they notice about the systems around them.

AOC’s official biography describes a life connected to the Bronx, a family move in search of stronger public schools, student loans, and work as a waitress and bartender after her father’s illness put the family home at risk. Those details alone can’t establish whether someone would be a good president or make one person representative of everyone who shares parts of that background. They can shape the questions a leader brings to power: Who can afford a home? Who gets heard? How can a school’s resources depend so much on a ZIP code?

Her record gives me something beyond biography to consider. She has advocated for public and affordable housing, and her current House work includes the Energy and Commerce Committee’s Energy, Health, and Oversight subcommittees. I’m drawn to politics that consider housing, health, climate, and people’s daily costs together. The test is whether proposals are workable, who benefits, who pays, and whether people affected have a voice.

I don’t think there is one set of “today’s American values.” We’re a diverse country, and people disagree. I believe many of us want leaders who listen, explain their choices, and make institutions answerable to ordinary people. AOC’s public voice and policy priorities give supporters grounds to argue that she could bring that perspective to the presidency. Voters should still test her plans, judgment, coalition-building, and ability to govern.

I’m not asking anyone to treat identity as proof or to agree with me. Her social location may offer a valuable perspective, her public record gives us something concrete to evaluate, and her ideas deserve a serious hearing. If she runs, I want to consider the full program carefully, and I’d be excited to support her.
