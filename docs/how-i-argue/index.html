About this blog

I'm Nick. This is my personal blog, where I share what I care about in social justice and what I've learned in school. Most of it is my opinion.

My background

Here's a little about where I've been, so you know where my opinions come from.

  • I studied psychology at San Diego State University, with a minor in Counseling and Social Change. Now I'm working toward a master's in Marriage and Family Therapy at Alliant International University.
  • For almost two years, I worked one on one with kids and their families as a behavior interventionist at Easterseals Southern California.
  • At San Diego State, I was a teaching assistant at the Children's Center, a research assistant in a lab that studies stress and bonding, and a student representative on the psychology department's DEI committee.
  • One summer, I volunteered at a women's resource center in Malvern, Pennsylvania. I kept a list of local resources and helped connect people with shelters.
  • In 2026, I joined the Kaiser Permanente Mental Health Scholars Academy for supervised training.

None of this makes me an expert. It's where I learned most of what I write about, and I'm still learning.

If you want the details, my full resume is on LinkedIn.

Where I start from

These are a few beliefs I keep coming back to. I borrowed them from a community nonprofit I look up to.

Neighbors look after neighbors.

Storms, layoffs, hunger and family crises hit whole towns at once. I start from the belief that we owe each other help when they do.

Help now, and a way forward.

Good help meets the urgent need today and leaves people more able to run their own lives tomorrow. I try to judge policies by both.

Watch for who falls through the cracks.

Every rule and program misses someone. I try to name who, and why.

Everyone has something to give.

Nobody is only a receiver. A good system leaves room for people to give back, in time, skill or care.

Start local.

Big questions land on real streets. Neighbors, local businesses, libraries, faith groups and volunteers are often the first to show up, and I want to learn from them.

These ideals borrow from Heart of the Ozarks, a community nonprofit in Springfield, Missouri, that pairs emergency help with tools for the long run. otohay blog is not affiliated with them.

A few things I try to do

I try to label my opinions.

When a piece is my view, it says so. Opinion pieces are marked on the index.

I try to link my sources.

Facts link to where they come from. Uncertain parts are marked as uncertain.

I try to make room for disagreement.

The strongest fair objection belongs inside the essay, not outside it.

I fix mistakes when I catch them.

If something turns out wrong, I correct it at the top of the post, with the date and what changed.

What this is not

Independent and unofficial. otohay blog is a personal project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or paid for by any politician, campaign, party, or political committee. Essays are opinion. Sourced claims are linked where they appear.

Most of these ideas aren't mine. I'm passing on what I learned from my professors at San Diego State.

Everything here is education and opinion. It is not therapy, diagnosis, or treatment, and it is not a substitute for a licensed professional.

Heads up: I write bluntly. If a post brings up something heavy, it's okay to stop reading.
