Why I think everyone should try therapy

What keeps people from trying therapy?

This is the script I read for one of my TikTok videos. I wrote it with AI help from published sources, checked the facts, and recorded it in my own voice. What I said on camera may differ a little.

Why I believe this

I think everyone should try therapy at least once. This one’s personal, so here’s why.

I’m a therapy grad student, and I’m still learning. So this is just me sharing what I believe.

I grew up in Philadelphia and moved to San Diego.

Therapists go too

Something that surprised me in school is that most therapists have been to therapy themselves. The American Psychological Association says about three out of four mental health professionals have. The people who know it best keep choosing it.

It also doesn’t have to take forever. The APA points to a classic study where half of people improved after eight sessions, and 75 percent improved after six months.

What gets in the way

A lot of us learned that therapy means something is wrong with you. I understand where that comes from. Many families were just trying to survive, and there wasn’t time or money to talk about feelings.

You don’t need a crisis to go. You can go because you’re stressed, grieving, stuck in the same argument, or you just want to know yourself better.

It also costs money and time, and I wish it were easier for everyone to get.

Making therapy feel normal

Here are three things I’m trying.

One. I say the word “therapy” out loud with friends and family, so it feels normal.

Two. When cost is the problem, I ask about sliding scale fees, community clinics, and university training clinics.

Three. I treat a first session like a first conversation. If it doesn’t feel like a fit, it’s okay to try someone else.

If you’re in crisis right now, you can call or text 988 in the U.S.
