Learning how to apologize, and how to move on

This is the script I read for one of my TikTok videos. I wrote it with AI help from published sources, checked the facts, and recorded it in my own voice. What I said on camera may differ a little.

Why repair matters

Nobody taught me how to apologize. And nobody taught me how to move on after.

I’m in therapy school, and I’m still bad at both. But here’s what I’ve been learning.

A psychologist named John Gottman studied couples for decades. The couples who lasted still fought. The difference was they repaired. Even babies and parents fall out of sync all the time, and researchers found that the repair is how trust gets built.

Guilt and shame

So why is it so hard? Brené Brown says guilt sounds like “I did something bad.” Shame sounds like “I am bad.”

Guilt can help us change. Shame makes us hide, or blame someone else.

A society that runs on shame

And our society runs on shame. One mistake can follow you forever online. Our justice system mostly asks how to punish someone.

Restorative justice asks different questions: Who was hurt? What do they need? Whose job is it to make it right?

That makes me angry, because people can grow when we give them a way back.

How I’m practicing sorry

Here are three things I’m trying.

One. Say sorry without a “but.” Name what I did and how it landed.

Two. Ask what would help make it right, and then actually do it.

Three. Once I’ve done my part, let myself move on. It still mattered. I just want to carry the lesson forward and leave the shame behind.
