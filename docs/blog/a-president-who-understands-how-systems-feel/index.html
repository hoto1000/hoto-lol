A President Who Understands How Systems Feel

Why I might support Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez if she runs for president in 2028: social location, her record, and a politics that connects housing, health, climate, and daily costs.

Oct 25, 2026 · 2 min read

Welcome to Project Hoto. This is my personal, unofficial commentary—not an AOC campaign message.

If Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez chooses to run for president in 2028, why might I support her? I’d start with social location: how a person’s place in society—shaped by family, class, neighborhood, education, work, and identity—can influence what they notice about the systems around them.

AOC’s official biography describes a life connected to the Bronx, a family move in search of stronger public schools, student loans, and working as a waitress and bartender after her father’s illness put the family home at risk. That background doesn’t automatically make anyone a good president, and it doesn’t mean one person speaks for everyone who shares parts of it. But it can inform the questions a leader brings to power: Who can afford a home? Who gets heard? How can a school’s resources depend so much on a ZIP code?

Her record gives us more than biography to consider. She has advocated for public and affordable housing, and her current House work includes the Energy and Commerce Committee’s Energy, Health, and Oversight subcommittees. I’m drawn to a politics that connects housing, health, climate, and people’s daily costs instead of treating them as unrelated problems. The real test is whether proposals are workable, who benefits, who pays, and whether people affected have a voice.

There isn’t one set of “today’s American values.” We’re a diverse country, and people disagree. But I believe many of us want leaders who listen, explain their choices, and make institutions answerable to ordinary people. AOC’s public voice and policy priorities give supporters a reason to argue she could bring that perspective to the presidency. Voters should still test her plans, judgment, coalition-building, and ability to govern.

I’m not asking anyone to treat identity as proof or to agree with me. I’m saying her social location may offer a valuable perspective, her public record gives us something concrete to evaluate, and her ideas deserve a serious hearing. If she runs, I’ll judge the full program—and I’d be excited to support her.
