Bronfenbrenner’s circles

Something I learned in therapy school that changed how I see the world. Part three.

A psychologist named Urie Bronfenbrenner drew a map of everything that raises a kid. He pictured it as circles. In the middle is the child. Around them are family, friends, and school.

Then come the things a kid never sees, like a parent’s job, the neighborhood, and local government. On the outside are the culture and the laws everyone lives under.

Rethinking blame

When I learned this, it changed how I think about blame. When a kid struggles, we usually look at the parents first. Bronfenbrenner’s map asks us to look at every circle. A parent can do everything they can and still be up against low wages, an underfunded school, or a two-hour bus ride to work.

Seeing every circle

I’m still learning to see all the circles. Here are three things I’m trying.

One. When a kid is struggling, I ask about every circle around them, beyond the home.

Two. I notice which circles I’m part of, as a neighbor, a coworker, or a voter.

Three. I pick one outer circle to show up for, like a school board meeting or a local budget hearing.

Check it yourself

  • Bronfenbrenner, U. (1979). The Ecology of Human Development.
