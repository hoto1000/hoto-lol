Why mental health matters

Why do so many of us wait years to get help?

N

By NickNov 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Ideas from Ronald Kessler, Philip Wang

This is the script I read for one of my TikTok videos. I wrote it with AI help from published sources, checked the facts, and recorded it in my own voice. What I said on camera may differ a little.

When it starts

Half of all mental health conditions start by age 14. And a lot of people wait years before they ever get help.

I’m a therapy grad student, and I’m still learning. So take this as me thinking out loud.

In 2005, Ronald Kessler and his team looked at a big national survey in the U.S. They found that half of all lifetime cases started by age 14, and three out of four started by age 24.

The same team looked at how long people waited. Among people who did get help, the wait was 6 to 8 years for depression and other mood conditions. For anxiety, it was 9 to 23 years.

That can be a whole childhood. Sometimes a whole young adulthood.

How common it is

And it’s common. The World Health Organization says about 1 in 8 people in the world were living with a mental health condition in 2019. In the U.S. in 2022, it was almost 1 in 4 adults, and only about half of them got treatment that year.

The WHO calls mental health an integral part of our general health. I think about that a lot. We wouldn’t tell someone to wait nine years to get a broken arm looked at.

Why people wait

It makes me angry that so many people carry this for years. A lot of it comes from shame, from cost, and from families who never had the words for it. I understand why. Many families were just trying to survive.

But I have hope, because I see it changing. The friend who asks a second time. The teacher who notices. The family that starts saying the word “therapy” out loud.

Talking about it plainly

Here are three things I’m trying.

One. Ask “how are you, really?” and actually wait for the answer.

Two. Talk about mental health the way I’d talk about a sore back, plainly and without shame.

Three. Keep 988 saved in my phone. If you or someone you love is in crisis, you can call or text 988 in the U.S.
